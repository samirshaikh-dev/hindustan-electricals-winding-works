Warning signs of failing bearings are a steady grinding or rumbling noise that gets louder with speed, increased motor body temperature near the end covers, visible play when you rock the shaft by hand, or vibration that is strongest at the bearing housings. If you ignore worn bearings, the rotor will start rubbing against the stator core and cause a costly winding burnout. We replace bearings with proper C3-clearance industrial grade bearings using a hydraulic press so the fit is correct and the bearing life is maximized.

Dynamic balancing corrects uneven weight distribution in a spinning rotor so it rotates without heavy vibration. It is required when you feel strong motor vibration, after a bent or machined shaft, whenever bearings have failed catastrophically, or after rewinding a high-speed or large motor. Unbalanced rotors destroy bearings in weeks and eventually crack the motor end shields and machine mountings. We balance rotors in two planes and correct unbalance by adding or removing weight so the motor runs smooth after installation.

After the new coils are placed in the slots, the winding is vacuum-impregnated or dipped in high-grade electrical varnish and then oven-baked at a controlled temperature. This locks every conductor firmly in place, prevents coil movement and vibration that would wear the enamel, fills air gaps that can cause corona and partial discharge, improves heat transfer out of the winding, and adds a second layer of moisture and chemical protection. Skipping proper varnishing is the most common reason a freshly rewound motor fails within a few months.

Frequent tripping tells you the motor is drawing more current than it should, and the most common reasons are: actual mechanical overload on the machine, low or unbalanced supply voltage, a phase that is partially open (single phasing), shorted turns inside the winding, or a mechanically seized load. Bypassing the protection will burn the winding and turn a small problem into a full rewinding job.

Single phasing happens when one of the three supply lines is lost because of a blown fuse, a loose connection, a faulty contactor pole, or a broken cable. A 3-phase induction motor running on two phases will draw very high current in the remaining two windings and overheat rapidly, usually burning the winding within minutes if the protection does not trip. Always switch off the motor immediately and check all three supply phases, the fuses, the contactor, and the cable joints before attempting to restart.

In submersible motors, delayed tripping is usually caused by moisture seeping into the winding through a damaged cable joint or a worn mechanical seal, by silt and sand locking the impeller, or by an internal winding fault whose resistance drops as the winding gets hot. Low borewell water level causing the motor to run dry is another very common cause. We test the stator with a Megger to confirm ground leakage, rewind the motor with moisture-resistant Class F/H insulation, and advise on seal and cable replacement so the same problem does not repeat.
